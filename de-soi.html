UX/UI case study · ecommerce ritual

DE SOI

Making the non-alcoholic aperitif feel like a beautiful evening choice.

A portfolio case study exploring how De Soi can translate French aperitif culture, botanical wellness, and sober-curious commerce into a warm, cinematic shopping experience.
Role · UX/UI + visual direction Timeline · 4-6 weeks Scope · research to final UI

Emotional lens

De Soi is not only selling a drink. It is selling permission to unwind differently.

Brand feeling

Warm aperitif culture, botanical softness, sober clarity, and table-side pleasure.

Design goal

Make the product feel premium and desirable before explaining the functional benefits.

Problem

How might we make a sober choice feel indulgent, social, and worth purchasing online?

Perception gap

Non-alcoholic products can be read as a compromise. De Soi needed to feel like an intentional pleasure.

Education gap

Users need to understand adaptogens, flavor, pack size, subscriptions, and occasion without being slowed down.

Ritual gap

The site had to communicate taste and atmosphere before the user can pour, smell, or sip anything.

Research

The strongest signal was not wellness. It was ritual.

People buy the occasion

The emotional hook is what remains: glassware, color, dinner, decompression, hosting, and taste.

Wellness needs softness

Adaptogens matter, but the language should feel botanical and calm, never clinical.

Commerce needs confidence

Flavor notes, press, reviews, pack types, and subscription savings reduce hesitation.

Luxury needs restraint

The interface should feel premium through pacing, hierarchy, and texture.

UX Strategy

Lead with feeling. Support with proof. Convert with calm.

A cinematic first sip

Open with mood, not inventory. Let the brand feel like an evening before asking the user to shop.

Flavor as navigation

Let users browse by taste, color, occasion, and format instead of a generic product grid.

Function after desire

Move adaptogen education into supportive moments where it deepens trust instead of interrupting appetite.

Checkout without pressure

Keep cart, subscription, and pack selection familiar so emotion never creates friction.

Structure rhythm

Hero ritual Best sellers Flavor story Adaptogens Press proof Cart path

Content hierarchy

Brand promise, product discovery, ingredient reassurance, cultural validation, and purchase paths.

Navigation logic

Keep primary paths direct: shop, subscribe, learn, find in store. Let story support the decision.

Wireframes

Wireframes were used to test pacing before visual seduction took over.

Homepage

Tested how quickly product should appear after the emotional opening.

Product page

Placed flavor, pack size, subscription, ingredients, and reviews around add-to-cart.

Mobile

Condensed editorial sections into touch-friendly modules and sticky decision points.

UI System

A system that lets product color lead while the interface stays composed.

Typography

Serif moments create appetite; compact utility text keeps commerce clear.

Color

Navy, burgundy, citrus, lilac, green, and cream let flavor become visible.

Cards

Product modules behave like tasting cards with image, flavor, sensory note, and action.

Buttons

Primary actions stay clear. Secondary actions feel softer: discover, learn, find, subscribe.

Spacing

Generous but controlled spacing keeps the experience premium without feeling empty.

Accessibility

Readable text, clear contrast, predictable actions, and direct shopping paths.

Homepage Breakdown

The final experience is built like a slow scroll through an aperitif hour.

Product Experience

The product page should feel like reading a label, tasting a note, and deciding with confidence.

Sensory story

Flavor notes are treated as mood language: citrus, red fruit, pomegranate, ginger, vanilla, herbs.

Conversion clarity

Quantity, pack size, subscription savings, reviews, ingredients, and serving context sit near the purchase action.

Motion

The motion system should feel like a pour, not a performance.

Scroll reveals

Sections appear with a soft delay to create a slower luxury pace.

Product hover

Cards lift gently, like picking up a can from a table.

Pour moments

Short atmospheric loops can carry fizz, glass, and serving ritual.

Final Outcome

A commerce experience that feels like entering the first minutes of a dinner party.

The final concept positions De Soi as a sensory ritual: warm, social, botanical, premium, and clear enough to shop. The page does not ask users to give something up. It invites them to choose a different kind of pleasure.

Reflection

This project was a reminder that luxury UX is mostly pacing.

Emotion before explanation

Users need to feel the ritual before they care about the functional benefits.

Commerce still needs discipline

Atmosphere is only valuable when pricing, product format, and checkout remain effortless.

Wellness can be sensual

Botanical storytelling can be warm, social, and tactile.

Next steps

Prototype subscription flows, test flavor comparison cards, and validate ingredient education timing.
